# National School of Computer Science (ESI) — General Knowledge Base

## 1. General Information

The **National School of Computer Science (ESI)**, formerly known as the **National Institute of Computer Science (INI)**, is an Algerian higher education institution that trains **State Engineers in Computer Science**.

ESI is located in **Oued Smar**, approximately **15 km from the city center of Algiers, Algeria**.

ESI is a specialized **Grande École** dedicated to computer science and engineering. Its training combines theoretical foundations such as mathematics, algorithms, and systems with practical and applied areas such as software engineering, artificial intelligence, networks, and data science.

According to the provided information, ESI offers a collaborative and supportive environment with a strong focus on academic excellence. Student clubs and scientific activities also provide opportunities for technical, personal, and professional development.

---

## 2. History

ESI was originally founded as the **Center for Computer Science Studies and Research (CERI)** under the supervision of the former **Ministry of Planning and Regional Development**.

In **1984**, the institution was renamed and placed under the supervision of the **Ministry of Higher Education and Scientific Research**.

CERI was the **first specialized computer science training center in Africa**.

It trained the first State Engineers, Application Engineers, and Programmers in Algeria and also contributed to training students from several African countries, including:

- Benin
- Cameroon
- Gabon
- Mali
- Mauritania
- Senegal
- Tunisia
- and other African countries.

Historically, ESI/CERI/INI therefore had a role in computer science education extending beyond Algeria.

## 3. Admission

The National School of Computer Science offers a **preparatory cycle in Computer Science**, directly accessible after the **Baccalaureate**.

According to the provided information, admission places are reserved exclusively for **Algerian students** through the national admission system.

ESI is considered one of the most selective higher education institutions in Algeria.

Approximately **250 to 350 places** are available each year.

### 2025 Admission Averages

The reported admission averages for 2025 were:

| Baccalaureate stream | Admission average |
|---|---:|
| Mathematics | 18.19 / 20 |
| Science | 18.55 / 20 |
| Technical Mathematics | 18.93 / 20 |

According to the provided information, students with grades high enough to enter ESI represent approximately **1 in 1,000 Baccalaureate candidates**, or around **0.1%**.

## 4. Academic Environment

ESI provides a demanding academic environment with a strong focus on computer science and engineering.

The workload can be particularly demanding during the **preparatory cycle**. Students are expected to develop strong foundations in:

- Mathematics
- Algorithms
- Programming
- Computer Systems
- Networks
- Other areas of computer science

The curriculum progressively moves from fundamental concepts toward more applied subjects and projects.

According to the provided information, the environment is generally described as **motivational, enjoyable, collaborative, and supportive**. Students often work together and form study groups.

### Lectures, Tutorials, and Practicals

**Lecture (Cours):**  
A session where the professor explains the main concepts and theoretical foundations of a subject.

**Tutorial (TD — Travaux Dirigés):**  
A smaller session focused on exercises, problem solving, and applying concepts introduced during lectures.

**Practical (TP — Travaux Pratiques):**  
A hands-on session where students apply their knowledge through programming, technical exercises, or practical tasks.

## 5. Assessment and Exams

Assessment at ESI is not limited to one type of examination.

According to the provided information, students can be evaluated through:

- **CI — Contrôle Intermédiaire**
- **CF — Contrôle Final**
- **Graded TPs — TP notés**
- **Projects**
- **Interrogations**

### Contrôle Intermédiaire (CI)

The **Contrôle Intermédiaire (CI)** is comparable to the regular tests or homework-style assessments students may have experienced in high school.

It evaluates students during the progression of the course and helps assess their understanding before the final examination.

### Contrôle Final (CF)

The **Contrôle Final (CF)** is comparable to a **composition/exam** in the high-school system.

It generally evaluates a larger portion of the material covered in the subject.

### Graded TPs

Some practical sessions (**TP**) are graded.

These assessments are particularly relevant in computer science because they allow students to demonstrate their ability to apply theoretical concepts in practice.

### Projects

Projects may contribute to the student's assessment.

They allow students to work on practical problems and develop technical and teamwork skills.

### Interrogations

Students may also have **interrogations**, which are shorter assessments used to evaluate their understanding and preparation.

### Exam Period

In the preparatory cycle, students have a dedicated examination period for different modules. According to the provided information, exams typically last around **1.5 to 2 hours** and cover the material studied up to that point. Continuous assessments may also take place throughout the academic year.

### Replacement Exam (CR)

If a student has a **justified absence**, they may take a **replacement exam (CR)** according to the provided information.

## 6. Grades and Academic Performance

Getting good grades during the first semester is possible.

According to the provided information, many students have achieved grades of **15+/20** during their first semester through consistent effort and focus.

The **elimination grade is not fixed**. According to the provided information, it depends on the average grades of the students and is calculated as **60% of their average grades**.

It is also possible to **redo/repeat a year during the preparatory cycle**.

## 12. Negative Aspects and Challenges

Although ESI offers many opportunities, students may face several challenges.

### Heavy Workload

One of the main difficulties is the **academic workload**, especially during the preparatory years.

The workload can become overwhelming during:

- Examination periods
- Project deadlines
- Periods with several assessments

Managing studies alongside extracurricular activities can therefore be challenging.

### Pressure

Students may experience a significant amount of **academic pressure**, particularly when several assessments, projects, or deadlines occur close together.

### Slow Adaptation

Some students may need time to adapt to:

- The academic environment
- The workload
- A new city or campus
- New classmates and social surroundings
- The rhythm of preparatory studies

### Loneliness and Social Adjustment

Some students may struggle with **loneliness or social adjustment**, especially if they are far from home or are adapting to a large campus environment.

Students coming from outside Algiers may also experience:

- Homesickness
- Stress
- Difficulty adapting to a new environment

### Balancing Studies and Extracurricular Activities

Balancing academic work with clubs, sports, hobbies, and projects can be difficult when the workload is high.

Good organization, peer support, study groups, and participation in student activities can help students manage these challenges.

## 13. Study Methods and Techniques

Several study methods can help students improve their academic performance.

### Active Recall

Instead of simply rereading the course, students actively test themselves on the material.

### Spaced Repetition

Students review information at increasing intervals to strengthen long-term memory.

### Feynman Technique

Students explain a concept in simple terms as if they were teaching someone else.

This can help identify gaps in understanding.

### Pomodoro Technique

Study sessions are divided into focused periods separated by short breaks.

### Practice Problems

Students should regularly practice exercises and apply concepts rather than relying only on theoretical revision.
